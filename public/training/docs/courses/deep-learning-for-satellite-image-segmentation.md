## Course guide

Deep Learning for Satellite Image Segmentation is a certificate / intermediate-to-advanced programme built around 7 practical modules. Work through the modules in order; each one includes objectives, notes, a hands-on exercise, a knowledge check, and a deliverable.

### Learning outcomes

1. Use electromagnetic spectrum and platform and sensor characteristics to work with fundamentals of deep learning for computer vision and explain the result.
2. Use electromagnetic spectrum and platform and sensor characteristics to work with convolutional neural networks (cnns) for image analysis and explain the result.
3. Use electromagnetic spectrum and platform and sensor characteristics to work with u-net, deeplab, and segformer architectures and explain the result.
4. Use electromagnetic spectrum and platform and sensor characteristics to work with data preparation and augmentation for satellite imagery and explain the result.
5. Use electromagnetic spectrum and platform and sensor characteristics to work with training and fine-tuning segmentation models and explain the result.
6. Use electromagnetic spectrum and platform and sensor characteristics to work with model evaluation metrics (iou, f1-score, accuracy) and explain the result.

### Prerequisites

- Comfort with files, folders, and basic data tables.
- Introductory knowledge of the relevant tool or programming language.
- Willingness to complete short practical exercises after each module.

## Full module documents

### Module 01: Fundamentals of deep learning for computer vision

#### Learning objectives

- Explain fundamentals of deep learning for computer vision and where it fits within Deep Learning for Satellite Image Segmentation.
- Use a satellite scene viewer and band combinations and spectral indices to complete a small, documented workflow.
- Validate the result, record assumptions, and communicate the main finding clearly.

#### Key concepts

- electromagnetic spectrum
- platform and sensor characteristics
- spatial/spectral/temporal resolution
- image interpretation

#### Lesson notes

Fundamentals of deep learning for computer vision is best learned as a repeatable workflow: state the question, prepare the inputs, apply the method, check the output, and communicate the result. Keep the original data untouched and save each processing step so the work can be repeated.

The central ideas in this lesson are electromagnetic spectrum, platform and sensor characteristics, spatial/spectral/temporal resolution. Use a satellite scene viewer to demonstrate the idea, and use band combinations and spectral indices to confirm that the output behaves as expected.

Do not compare images from different dates or sensors without checking calibration, cloud cover, and spatial resolution.

#### Hands-on exercise

1. Write a one-sentence question and define the study area or sample extent for fundamentals of deep learning for computer vision.
2. Prepare the input data and record its source, date, resolution or scale, and coordinate system where relevant.
3. Apply the main technique with a satellite scene viewer: compare at least two band combinations, label three land-cover features, and explain how resolution affects interpretation.
4. Validate the result visually and numerically; note any unexpected patterns, missing values, or outliers.
5. Export the final map, table, or figure and write a short interpretation with limitations and next steps.

#### Knowledge check

<details>
<summary>Show questions and answers</summary>

1. **What does spatial resolution describe?**  
   Answer: The ground size of one pixel; smaller pixels mean finer spatial detail.

2. **Why are near-infrared and red bands useful for vegetation?**  
   Answer: Vegetation strongly reflects near-infrared and absorbs red, making their contrast useful for vegetation indices such as NDVI.

3. **What should be checked before comparing two satellite images?**  
   Answer: Date, sensor, calibration, cloud cover, projection, and spatial resolution.

</details>
