Master document preparation with LaTeX for academic and professional writing. Learn to create beautiful, structured documents with mathematical equations and scientific notation.

Course guide

Introduction to LaTeX is an introductory programme built around 8 practical modules. Work through the modules in order; each one includes objectives, notes, a hands-on exercise, a knowledge check, and a deliverable.

  • No prior programming experience is required for the introductory lessons.

01

LaTeX and its advantages

Learning objectives

  • Explain latex and its advantages and where it fits within Introduction to LaTeX.
  • Use LaTeX and a compiled .tex document to complete a small, documented workflow.
  • Validate the result, record assumptions, and communicate the main finding clearly.

Key concepts

document structuremath typesettingfloats for tables and figuresBibTeX references

Lesson notes

LaTeX and its advantages is best learned as a repeatable workflow: state the question, prepare the inputs, apply the method, check the output, and communicate the result. Keep the original data untouched and save each processing step so the work can be repeated.

The central ideas in this lesson are document structure, math typesetting, floats for tables and figures. Use LaTeX to demonstrate the idea, and use a compiled .tex document to confirm that the output behaves as expected.

Use labels and cross-references instead of hard-coded numbers so the document stays correct after edits.

Hands-on exercise

  1. Write a one-sentence question and define the study area or sample extent for latex and its advantages.
  2. Prepare the input data and record its source, date, resolution or scale, and coordinate system where relevant.
  3. Apply the main technique with LaTeX: create a short document with sections, one equation, one figure, one table, and at least two BibTeX citations.
  4. Validate the result visually and numerically; note any unexpected patterns, missing values, or outliers.
  5. Export the final map, table, or figure and write a short interpretation with limitations and next steps.
Knowledge check: 3 questions with answers
  1. What is the advantage of BibTeX over manual reference lists?
    Answer: Citations and the bibliography are generated consistently from one source file.
  2. How are figures usually handled in LaTeX?
    Answer: As float environments with captions, labels, and cross-references.
  3. Why avoid hard-coded section numbers?
    Answer: LaTeX renumbers automatically; manual numbers become wrong when content changes.
DeliverableA one-page practical note containing the question, data description, method steps, one output, and three bullet points of interpretation.

Introduction to LaTeX: Final Applied Project

The capstone consolidates the course into one applied, portfolio-ready piece of work.

  1. Choose a focused question related to the course theme and define the study area or sample dataset.
  2. Write a short project brief with data sources, intended method, and expected deliverables.
  3. Complete the processing workflow using at least three methods learned in this course.
  4. Validate the outputs and document every assumption, parameter, and limitation.
  5. Submit a final report with maps or figures, a reproducible workflow, and a concise recommendation.
Final submissionA report, reproducible workflow, maps or figures, and a five-minute presentation.
